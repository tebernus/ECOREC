What this page does. It solves one accounting model exactly, in your browser, and lets you change it. The model is a square system of seven accounts — production, two household groups, health care, financial intermediation and pollution — that are read as a single matrix. A policy shock is injected into some accounts and propagated through all of them by the inverse of (I − M).

The question. Does endogenising more of the accounting system change the answer to a policy question? The same shock is solved under three closures — an open 5×5 monetary core, a financially closed 6×6 core, and a fully coupled 7×7 system — and the results are shown side by side. Everything needed to read the experiment is on this page; the article is not required.

The example economy: seven accounts, five account families

The economy is deliberately small and round-numbered so that every number can be recomputed by hand. It is an illustrative benchmark, not an estimate for any country. Monetary accounts are in billions of currency units (Bn CU); the pollution account is in megatonnes of CO2 equivalent (Mt CO2e).

The seven accounts map onto the five account families of the framework: SNA (A1, A2), distributional SAM (H1, H2), health (Hc), flow of funds (Ff) and environmental satellite (Pp). The full benchmark of the framework — GDP reconciliation, flow-of-funds matrix, SEEA flows, health accounts and quintile distribution — reconciles at 1,000.0 Bn CU of GDP; this seven-account reduction is its structural core.

The model: one matrix, three closures, one inverse

Let mij be the payment that account j makes to account i per unit of j's total outlays. Column j of the coefficient matrix M is therefore the expenditure pattern of account j, and the accounting system reads

y = M y + x   ⟹   y = (I − M)−1 x

where y is the vector of account totals and x the vector of exogenous injections. Entry (i, j) of the inverse is the total effect on account i of a one-unit injection into account j, after all rounds of the circular flow. Because

(I − M)−1 = I + M + M2 + M3 + …

the multiplier can be read round by round, and the series converges if and only if the spectral radius satisfies the Hawkins–Simon condition ρ(M) < 1. The badge in the header reports ρ(M) computed from the characteristic polynomial, not estimated.

The three closures. Each adds accounts to the endogenous block of the inverse.
ClosureAccountsWhat is endogenousWhat leaks
open5×5 A1, A2, H1, H2, Hc. Conventional Leontief–SAM core. Household saving (10% and 30%) and imports; pollution is a passive row outside the inverse.
financially closed6×6 The five above plus Ff: saving is intermediated into capital formation. 20% of intermediated funds goes abroad; pollution still passive.
coupled7×7 All six above plus Pp: pollution responds to activity and feeds back on income, health spending and output. Imports and the foreign leakage of funds.

The coupled column (account Pp)

One additional megatonne of emissions has three effects, expressed in Bn CU per Mt and read down the pollution column: it destroys δH1 of low-income earnings (occupational and environmental exposure), δH2 of high-income earnings, and it raises defensive curative health spending by φ. Those are the entries that make the seventh diagonal of the inverse fall below one and the environmental feedback self-damping. The pollution column is a vector of marginal responses, not a vector of expenditure shares, so it does not sum to one.

Household columns under a change of saving rate

A column of M must keep summing to one when a saving rate changes, or the accounting would no longer balance. The explorer therefore rescales the consumption propensities of each household column by

αH1 = (1 − sH1) / 0.90,     αH2 = max(0, 0.95 − sH2) / 0.65

so that the low-income column spends 0.90αH1 on goods and health and saves sH1, and the high-income column spends 0.65αH2, transfers 0.05 to H1 and saves sH2. Both columns then total exactly 1.000 at every admissible saving rate, which the diagnostics panel verifies live.

The other emission intensities are fixed: services 0.10 Mt per Bn CU, health care 0.05, low-income outlays 0.15, high-income outlays 0.20, finance 0.00. Over the whole admissible box of the six structural parameters the spectral radius stays below 1 (its maximum, 0.987, occurs at high emissions intensity, high health burden and zero morbidity drags), so the multiplier is finite for every setting the sliders allow. The header badge turns red if a configuration ever violates the condition.

The four scenarios

Each scenario is a 10.0 Bn CU injection, except that Policy D also lowers the emissions intensity of industry to 0.25 Mt per Bn CU. The shock vector is ordered (A1, A2, H1, H2, Hc, Ff, Pp); entries not listed are zero.

A preset sets the four injections and, for Policy D, the emissions intensity. The other five structural parameters are left exactly as you have set them, so a preset reproduces the published columns only at their baseline values.

How to read the output
  • Headline cards report the coupled 7×7 solution for the current shock and parameters, with the difference from the financially closed 6×6 counterfactual.
  • Closure comparison solves the same shock under all three closures. The last column is the coupled value as a percentage of the 6×6 value: it isolates what environmental endogenisation adds.
  • Two charts: the first compares the three closures account by account; the second splits the coupled response into the direct injection, the first round of re-spending and everything after it.
  • Round-by-round table shows Mκx for rounds 0 to 6, the geometric tail that remains, and the exact total. It is the numerical content of the power-series representation.
  • Matrix panel shows the live coefficient matrix M or the multiplier matrix (I − M)−1 for any of the three closures. Negative cells are shaded red; diagonal cells blue. Hover or tap a cell to read its row and column.
  • Reference results are the baseline calibration of the article, recomputed at load: 11 closure–scenario combinations. They do not move when the sliders move.

Every figure is a total effect of the stated injection, obtained as the appropriate column combination of (I − M)−1. Emissions for the 5×5 and 6×6 closures are the passive pollution row applied to the solution; for the 7×7 they are the seventh element of the solution itself, which is why the coupled figure can be lower than the financially closed one.

The Explorer — coupled multiplier for the example economy of the unified accounting framework. Single file, no network access, no external library. The numerical engine inverts the coefficient matrix by Gauss–Jordan elimination with partial pivoting, obtains ρ(M) from the roots of the characteristic polynomial, and checks the inversion against the identity matrix on every update.
